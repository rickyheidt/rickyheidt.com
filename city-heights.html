A large, central San Diego neighborhood with older bungalows, duplexes and small multifamily homes along busy commercial corridors.

Living in City Heights

City Heights is one of the larger neighborhoods in central San Diego, spread across a mesa between Kensington, Normal Heights, North Park and Southeastern San Diego, and it includes smaller neighborhoods such as Teralta and Fairmount Village. Its main commercial corridors are El Cajon Boulevard, University Avenue and Fairmount Avenue, with a broad mix of independent restaurants, markets and shops.

Public spaces include Colina del Sol Park, Teralta Park and the Weingart Library and Performance Annex. The neighborhood is served by many bus routes and sits close to SR-15, SR-94 and I-805, so most of central San Diego is a short drive away.

Homes you'll find

  • Spanish and Craftsman bungalows: on residential streets, many on lots with room to improve.
  • Mid-century homes: mixed in across the neighborhood.
  • Duplexes and small multifamily properties: popular with owner occupants and investors.
  • Newer townhomes and infill projects: near the main corridors.
  • Homes with detached garages or large lots: where an ADU may be possible, subject to city rules.

Getting around and schools

City Heights is served by San Diego Unified School District, which also offers choice and magnet programs. Assignments depend on the exact address, so confirm directly with the district.

Many bus routes serve El Cajon Boulevard and University Avenue, and SR-15, SR-94 and I-805 are close. Errands along the corridors are walkable, and street parking varies from block to block.

Buying in City Heights

Look carefully at the age and condition of the home and any additions or conversions. Ask for permit records on garages, extra units and room additions, and have an inspector check foundations, plumbing, electrical systems and roofing.

If you plan to add an ADU or rent part of the property, ask the city about the specific lot and the rules on setbacks, parking and utilities. State law has made ADUs and, in some cases, lot splits easier, but eligibility depends on the property, so get answers before you build them into your budget.

Selling in City Heights

City Heights buyers include owner occupants, investors and people planning to add units. Lead with lot size, the garage or any accessory structure, and the permit history. If the property produces rental income, share leases and rent rolls with serious buyers.

Good photography and a clear description of the layout help owner occupant buyers picture daily life. Show gardens, trees and any updates.

City Heights FAQ

Where is City Heights?

In central San Diego, between Kensington, Normal Heights and Southeastern San Diego, along El Cajon Boulevard and University Avenue.

What types of homes are in City Heights?

Bungalows, mid-century homes, duplexes, small multifamily properties and newer townhomes.

Can I add an ADU in City Heights?

Possibly, depending on the lot. Ask the city about setbacks, parking and permits for the specific address.

What should investors look at in City Heights?

Permit history for extra units, rent rolls and leases, the condition of older systems and the rules on adding or legalizing units.
